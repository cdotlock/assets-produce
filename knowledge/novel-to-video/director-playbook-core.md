# Director Playbook Core

## 1. Use Shot Size To Carry Emotion

Dialogue and inner-conflict scenes work best as a progression:

- wide or medium-wide: establish space and power positions
- medium-close: isolate conflict
- close-up: reserve for the decisive emotional moment

Do not force a whole three-stage arc into one 12-second shot. Identify which stage this shot owns.

## 2. Multi-Character Staging

For three or more characters, avoid a flat line. Use Z-depth:

- foreground: current emotional subject
- midground: counterforce or witness
- background: pressure, exit, authority, or observer

Give each named character a screen zone and facing direction.

## 3. Stable Motion Over Ambitious Motion

Prefer stable physical moves: step, turn, grip, release, kneel, enter, exit, stop.

For fast or chaotic motion, create motion through environment and camera:

- subject mostly stable
- hair/clothes/light movement
- background blur or side light streaks
- subtle camera shake only when needed

## 4. Three-Part Stillness Ending

Every important shot should end with:

1. camera still
2. characters still
3. composition locked until the video ends

This is a technical continuity guard as much as a dramatic choice.

## 5. Silence For Major Turns

Truth reveal, betrayal, fatal decision, and ritual pause should use reduced sound:

- music stops or thins
- room tone drops
- one small source remains, such as clock/fireplace/footstep

Avoid stacking dramatic music, heavy effects, and dialogue at the same time.

## 6. Entrances Need Paths

When a character enters from offscreen, specify:

- entry edge or door
- route through the frame
- obstacle avoidance
- final position

Use the same screen-zone terms as the spatial map.

## 7. Camera Angle Words Need Physical Detail

Prefer physical camera descriptions over abstract labels:

- "camera over Sylvia's left shoulder toward James" instead of just "OTS"
- "camera at knee height, slowly rising to face height" instead of just "low angle"
- "horizon tilts about 12 degrees" instead of just "Dutch angle"

## 8. L4 Action Chain Guard

For collapse, fight, fall, dizziness, or sudden impact shots:

- single shot should usually be 6 seconds or less
- keep major actions to four or fewer
- choose split points at natural still states
- keep audio extremely quiet to reduce audio-side safety failures in live generation
